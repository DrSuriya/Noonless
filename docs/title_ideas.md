# Title ideas

The title is still open (**D14** in `docs/design.pdf`). Nothing here is a
decision. When one is made, record it in `docs/decisions.md`. Until then, the
builds use the working name "Horror maze prototype".

## What the title should keep

- The **sun dial**: the currency, and the motif that a sundial is useless
  without the sun.
- The idea of **escaping the system to see the sky** again (Section 2.1).

## Ligne de fuite / line of flight

A concept from the French philosophers Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari. The
term comes from perspective drawing, where the *lignes de fuite* run to the
vanishing point. Philosophically it means escaping a rigid system through its
own cracks, not by running into nothing but by turning into something new
that the system can no longer track. *Fuite* also means "leak".

Why it fits the game:

- **The dead end is the vanishing point.** A long corridor narrows to a
  vanishing point, and that's where the dead end sits. The swap fires when the
  player stares at it, so the line of flight is both what the player looks at
  and how they get out.
- **Escape through the system's own cracks.** Dead ends are the maze's
  defense and the player's way out: the design's thesis.
- **Turning into something new.** Each swap throws the player into another
  world; the collapse rearranges the map; maps leave the pool.
- **It meets the sky motif at the horizon.** In perspective drawing the
  vanishing points sit on the horizon line, which is where the sky begins. A
  line of flight runs toward the horizon, toward the sky the player wants to
  see again. The sun dial would still need to live in the lore, the currency
  or a subtitle.

Where it pulls against the design:

- Deleuze and Guattari's escape works by mutating, not by destroying the
  system, while the player breaks the simulation by destroying its objectives
  (Section 2.1). Worth weighing when the lore is decided (**D13**).

### Further reading

- Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari, *A Thousand Plateaus* (1980; English
  translation by Brian Massumi, 1987). The main source; the opening plateau,
  "Introduction: Rhizome", introduces lines of flight. Dense.
- Gilles Deleuze and Claire Parnet, *Dialogues* (1977), the chapter "On the
  Superiority of Anglo-American Literature". The most readable account of
  escape as a line of flight.
- Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari, *Kafka: Toward a Minor Literature*
  (1975). The same idea worked through Kafka, whose burrows and castles are
  mazes of their own; some translations say "line of escape".
- Introductions: Brian Massumi, *A User's Guide to Capitalism and
  Schizophrenia* (1992), and Claire Colebrook, *Gilles Deleuze* (Routledge
  Critical Thinkers, 2002).
